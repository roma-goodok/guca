How to read the map

Each point is one organism form. Its height follows atlas fitness. Larger circles show forms that appear in more runs. The legend explains the ring colors. Horizontal distance does not measure similarity.

Each arrow shows a change of champion between consecutive checkpoints in one run or island. Parent-child relationships require separate evidence. If a filter hides a form, the map omits its arrows. The atlas uses saved champions and archive records.

Research notes & definitions

The data covers a sample of forms. A repeated form can persist between snapshots. Its unseen duration is unknown.

Four q6.35 restarts form one declared unit with four separate histories. For each arrow, the source form determines the denominator.

P(B | departure from A) = count(A → B) / Σ count(A → C), C ≠ A. A form needs an observed departure to have an outgoing probability. Sampling cadence can hide intermediate forms. Repeated events can come from the same experimental unit.

The atlas matches forms by graph structure, including disconnected components. Vertex numbers and states can vary within one form. The default drawing shows one observed example. Source records show their own saved states. The atlas uses individual scores.

Equalized height combines 30% normalized raw fitness and 70% normalized mean rank across all topology classes. Equal scores share a height. Filters keep the scale fixed. Axis labels, thresholds and exports use raw fitness.

Champion, archive and historical-record roles stay separate. One topology at one run, island and generation counts once, even if multiple sources identify it. Visits restart after a different observed form or a history gap.
